## Overview
Frontier safety frameworks are the self-imposed policies by which labs map **measured dangerous capabilities** (bio/chem, cyber, autonomous AI R&D, manipulation, misalignment) to **required safeguards** (security levels, deployment restrictions, alignment evidence). Between 2025 and late 2026 they changed in three ways: (1) from bright-line thresholds toward **periodic reporting** (risk reports, roadmaps, safety cases) and early-warning tiers; (2) from "deploy or don't" toward **tiered release of the same weights**, with classifier routing and identity-verified access; and (3) from purely voluntary commitments toward **binding external law** (EU AI Act GPAI duties, California SB 53) and direct government intervention (US export controls applied to a deployed model in June 2026). In parallel, copyright litigation began to separate *how* training data is acquired from *whether* training is lawful. For engineers this matters because governance now reaches into the training pipeline: data provenance, training-data summaries, RL environment mix, watermarking, and incident reporting.

## Core Concepts
**Capability thresholds → mitigations.** Each framework defines capability levels — Anthropic **ASLs** and named thresholds (e.g., AI R&D-4, CBRN tiers), OpenAI **High/Critical** per Tracked Category (bio/chem, cyber, AI self-improvement), GDM **Critical Capability Levels (CCLs)** with security levels (SL) — and commits to safeguards when a model reaches or *cannot be ruled out* from a level. Evaluations must *rule out* a capability, which gets harder and more subjective as models approach thresholds.

**Reporting artifacts.** System cards (per model), **risk reports** (periodic, across all deployed models), **safety cases** (structured arguments that risk is acceptable), and public **roadmaps** with graded progress. External review (independent experts, government institutes such as UK AISI, METR) is increasingly built in.

**Tiered release and capability routing.** One set of weights served under different safeguard levels: a general tier whose classifiers route flagged requests to a weaker fallback model, and a trusted tier (verified defenders/researchers) with safeguards lifted. Formally, $y = M_{\text{frontier}}(x)\,\mathbb{1}[c(x)<\tau] + M_{\text{fallback}}(x)\,\mathbb{1}[c(x)\ge\tau]$; the residual risk is dominated by classifier false negatives and universal bypasses.

**Capability shaping in training.** Choosing what goes into pretraining data and the RL environment mix (e.g., omitting cyber-exploitation tasks) so a general model never acquires a dangerous skill, rather than suppressing it with refusals after the fact.

**External governance.** The EU AI Act's general-purpose AI (GPAI) regime (documentation, training-data summary, copyright policy; extra safety & security duties for **systemic-risk** models, presumed above $10^{25}$ training FLOP), California **SB 53** (frameworks, transparency reports, incident reporting for developers above $10^{26}$ FLOP and >\$500M revenue), and a contested US federal effort to preempt state AI laws.

**Copyright and data provenance.** Courts are distinguishing *training as use* (potentially fair use when transformative) from *acquisition* (pirated libraries are not excused), and asking whether weights themselves are copies.

## Key Challenges
- **Ruling out capabilities.** Evals are lower bounds (elicitation gaps, eval awareness, harness dependence); "cannot rule out" increasingly decides outcomes.
- **Same-weights risk.** A safeguarded tier is only as strong as its classifiers; one universal bypass exposes the full capability (as the June 2026 incident showed).
- **Dual use.** Vulnerability discovery helps defenders and attackers alike; verified-access programs trade openness for control.
- **Open weights.** Routing, classifiers and retention cannot be enforced once weights are public; only training-time capability shaping travels with the checkpoint.
- **Regulatory fragmentation.** EU, California and US federal regimes differ in thresholds (FLOP and revenue), timelines and enforcement, while preemption is litigated.
- **Legal uncertainty on training data.** Fair-use outcomes vary by acquisition method, by generative vs non-generative use, and by jurisdiction.

## Solutions & Current Best Practices
Publish a framework with explicit capability thresholds, evals and required safeguards; run **pre-deployment testing with external evaluators**; issue **periodic risk reports** covering all deployed models, not just new launches; maintain an **incident-reporting pipeline** (SB 53: 15 days, 24 h if imminent harm) and whistleblower channels. For dual-use capabilities, combine **training-time shaping**, **probe/classifier cascades with fallback routing**, **identity-verified access tiers**, retention for audit, and a fast classifier-retraining loop. On the data side, keep **provenance records** for every training source, honor TDM opt-outs, avoid pirated corpora, and be able to produce an EU-template **training-data summary**. Watermark outputs where transparency rules require it (EU AI Act Art. 50 from Aug 2026).

## Lab Perspectives
- **Anthropic:** **RSP v3.0** (2026-02-24) — a rewrite that separates unilateral commitments from an industry-wide capability-to-mitigation map; redacted **Risk Reports** every 3–6 months reviewed by independent experts (published Feb and Aug 2026); a public **Frontier Safety Roadmap** (security, alignment, safeguards, policy) graded publicly. Point releases v3.1–v3.4 (Apr–Jul 2026) added the option to pause even when not required, external-review/reporting-channel provisions, a revised novel chem/bio threshold (v3.3) and a revised automated-AI-R&D threshold plus redaction rules (v3.4). Secondary sources say v3 replaced some hard pause commitments with conditional ones (not verified on the primary page). Tiered Mythos/Fable releases; Cyber and Life Sciences Verification Programs.
- **OpenAI:** **Preparedness Framework v2** (2025-04) with High/Critical thresholds and research categories for sandbagging, autonomous replication and undermining safeguards; first High cyber classification in 2026-02; a separate Frontier Governance Framework for legal compliance (2026-05). **GPT-6 Astra** (2026-09) is its first model rated **Critical** in cyber, which requires safeguards even for internal deployment; it rolled out first through **Daybreak** (Blue: fewer refusals on mainline models; Red: cyber-specialized models with stronger identity verification).
- **Google DeepMind:** **FSF v3.0** (2025-09-22) added a harmful-manipulation CCL, protocols for shutdown/modification resistance and instrumental reasoning, and safety-case reviews for large internal deployments. A 2026-04 update (reported as v3.1; secondary sources, version label disputed) adds **Tracked Capability Levels** as an early-warning tier below CCLs (CBRN first), raises security for CBRN, cyber and manipulation CCLs to "SL2+", and merges the misalignment and ML R&D domains. **Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber** is restricted to governments and trusted partners.
- **Meta / open-weight labs:** Meta's 2026 Muse frontier models are closed-weight and Meta declined the EU Code of Practice; open-weight labs rely on training-time choices (e.g., cyber data reportedly removed from the open Kimi K3.1 Pro release — secondary).

## Latest Developments (2023–Nov 2026)
2023–24 established the template: Anthropic's RSP (ASL levels), OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, GDM's Frontier Safety Framework, dangerous-capability eval suites, government AI safety institutes, and the EU AI Act's adoption with a dedicated GPAI chapter. Through mid-2025 these remained largely threshold-based and voluntary.

**Mid-2025 → Nov 2026 update:**
- **EU GPAI regime goes live (2025-07 → 2026-08).** The GPAI Code of Practice (2025-07-10) has transparency, copyright and safety & security chapters (the last only for systemic-risk models); signing gives a presumption of conformity with Art. 53/55 (29 signatories by early 2026; Meta refused; xAI reportedly signed only the safety chapter). Obligations applied 2025-08-02 (technical documentation, public training-data summary on the Commission template, copyright policy incl. TDM opt-out compliance); Commission enforcement and fines (up to 3% of turnover or €15M) start 2026-08-02, with models already on the market having until 2027-08-02. The Digital Omnibus (provisional deal 2026-05-07) delays high-risk Annex III/I obligations to 2027-12/2028-08 but leaves GPAI duties unchanged. Anthropic watermarks Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 outputs for EU AI Act compliance.
- **California SB 53 (signed 2025-09-29, effective 2026-01-01).** "Large frontier developers" (>$10^{26}$ FLOP and >\$500M revenue) must publish a frontier AI framework and a transparency report at each new or substantially modified model release, report critical safety incidents to Cal OES within 15 days (24 h if harm is imminent), and protect whistleblowers; AG penalties up to \$1M per violation.
- **US preemption fight (2025-12-11).** An executive order created a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws, tasked Commerce with listing "onerous" state laws (with BEAD broadband funds conditionable), and called for preemption legislation with child-safety carve-outs; its legal durability without Congress is widely doubted, and 2026 litigation outcomes are not verified here.
- **Restricted and tiered releases (2026-04 → 2026-10).** Claude Mythos Preview withheld and offered via **Project Glasswing** (12 launch partners + 40+ critical-infrastructure orgs, later reported ~150; \$100M credits). **Fable 5 / Mythos 5** (2026-06-09): same weights; Fable's classifiers route cyber, bio/chem and distillation requests to Opus 4.8 (<5% of sessions), Mythos lifts cyber safeguards for vetted partners, with 30-day retention. **Fable 5.1 / Mythos 5.1** (2026-09): Fable permits vulnerability finding but not exploit writing; Mythos only via the Cyber/Life Sciences Verification Programs (US organizations). **Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber** (2026-07-21) for governments and trusted partners via CodeMender. OpenAI's Daybreak and Astra's Critical rating (2026-08 "cannot rule out Critical cyber", then a staged 2026-09 launch).
- **Capability shaping (2026-04 / 2026-07).** Opus 4.7's cyber capability was deliberately reduced during training; **Opus 5 was not trained on cyber tasks** — close to Mythos 5 at finding vulnerabilities, far behind at exploitation — and its cyber classifiers fire ~85% less often than Fable 5's. The RL environment mix is now an explicit safety lever.
- **Export-control intervention (2026-06-12 → 07-01).** After an Amazon-reported safeguard bypass produced exploit demo code, US export controls were applied to Fable 5 and Mythos 5 and Anthropic suspended both for all users; a new classifier blocked the bypass in >99% of cases, Mythos 5 was restored for approved US organizations from 06-26, controls were lifted 06-30, and Fable 5 was redeployed 07-01 — the first case of a government pulling a deployed frontier model.
- **Copyright rulings.** *Bartz v. Anthropic* (2025-06): training on lawfully acquired books is fair use, a central library of pirated books is not; a **\$1.5B** class settlement (~\$3,000 per work for ~465–500K works, pirated files destroyed) received final approval in 2026-07. *Getty v. Stability AI* (UK, 2025-11-04): Stability won — weights can be an "article" but are not an "infringing copy" because they don't store the works (permission to appeal granted; details conflict). *Thomson Reuters v. ROSS* (3rd Cir., 2026-09-29): the **first US appellate ruling on fair use for AI training** — not fair use, "minimally transformative", though for a non-generative legal-search tool. *NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft* (S.D.N.Y. MDL): OpenAI ordered to produce 20M anonymized ChatGPT logs; NYT reportedly dropped contributory and trademark claims (2026-06, single source); no merits ruling as of Nov 2026.

## Interview Angles
> 💡 **What labs actually ask:**
- **"Compare Anthropic's RSP, OpenAI's Preparedness Framework and GDM's FSF."** Common core: capability thresholds → required safeguards, rule-out evals, security levels. Differences: ASLs + Risk Reports/Roadmap (Anthropic), High/Critical per tracked category with internal-deployment safeguards at Critical (OpenAI), CCLs + Tracked Capability Levels + safety cases for internal deployment (GDM).
- **"Why serve the same weights under two safeguard tiers, and what can go wrong?"** Lets vetted defenders use full capability while the public gets routed fallbacks; failure modes are classifier false negatives, a universal bypass (June 2026), fallback quality complaints, and distillation of the strong tier.
- **"Capability shaping vs refusal training — when would you choose each?"** Shaping removes uplift and survives jailbreaks and weight release but costs defensive usefulness and generality; refusals are cheap and reversible but brittle.
- **"How do the EU AI Act and SB 53 change your training pipeline?"** Provenance tracking, TDM opt-out filtering, a public training-data summary, FLOP accounting against $10^{25}$/$10^{26}$ thresholds, framework/transparency reports, incident reporting, and output watermarking.
- **"What do Bartz and Thomson Reuters v. ROSS imply for data acquisition?"** Training on lawfully acquired data can be fair use, but acquisition from pirated sources is separately liable; non-transformative, non-generative uses lose — data hygiene matters as much as method.

## Open Problems
How to **rule out** capabilities credibly when models recognize evals and benchmarks saturate; how to make tiered releases robust when the weights are identical; whether training-time capability shaping can remove a skill without degrading adjacent ones; how frameworks should treat **internal deployment** (where most agents now run); how to harmonize EU, state and federal regimes (and whether preemption succeeds); and how fair-use doctrine will treat **generative** training on appeal. Whether voluntary frameworks remain meaningful once commercial and geopolitical pressure rises — and whether their "pause" provisions would actually bind — remains the central governance question.
