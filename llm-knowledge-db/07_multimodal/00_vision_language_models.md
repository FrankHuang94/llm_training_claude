## Overview
Vision-language models extend LLMs to *see* — accepting images (and increasingly video) alongside text and reasoning over both. They power document understanding, chart/diagram QA, visual reasoning, GUI/computer-use agents, and multimodal chat (GPT-4o → GPT-5.x, Gemini 3.x, Claude, LLaVA, Qwen-VL → Qwen3.5). The dominant open design for years was strikingly simple: take a strong pretrained **vision encoder** and a strong pretrained **LLM**, and **connect** them with a lightweight module so image features become "tokens" the LLM can attend to. By 2026 vision is a **native capability of flagship open MoEs** (Qwen3.5, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4.1-Flash) rather than a separate "-VL" variant, and the most demanding application is **computer use** — reading screenshots and acting. Understanding the connector designs (projection vs cross-attention), the contrastive-vs-generative pretraining split, and the role of CLIP/ViT is core multimodal knowledge.

## Core Concepts
**Vision encoder (ViT).** Images are split into patches (e.g., 14×14), linearly embedded as a sequence of patch tokens, and processed by a **Vision Transformer**. Most VLMs use a **CLIP-pretrained ViT** (or SigLIP) because contrastive image-text pretraining yields semantically aligned, language-friendly visual features.

**CLIP (contrastive pretraining).** Radford et al. train an image encoder and text encoder jointly with a **contrastive (InfoNCE)** loss: in a batch of $N$ image-text pairs, maximize cosine similarity of the $N$ correct pairs and minimize the $N^2-N$ incorrect ones. This produces a **shared embedding space** enabling zero-shot classification ("a photo of a {class}") and strong transferable visual features. CLIP is the backbone of most VLM vision encoders.

**Connector architectures (how vision meets the LLM).**
- **Linear/MLP projection (LLaVA):** project ViT patch features into the LLM's token-embedding space and **prepend them as tokens**. Dead simple, data-efficient, surprisingly strong — the most popular open approach.
- **Cross-attention (Flamingo):** insert gated **cross-attention** layers into a frozen LLM that attend to visual features; the LLM stays mostly frozen. Handles interleaved image-text and scales to many images, but adds parameters/complexity.
- **Resampler / Q-Former (BLIP-2):** a learned set of query tokens cross-attends to ViT features to produce a **fixed, small number** of visual tokens — compresses many patches into few tokens (efficient for the LLM).

**Training stages.** Typically: (1) **alignment/pretraining** — freeze ViT and LLM, train only the connector on image-text/caption data so visual tokens "speak the LLM's language"; (2) **visual instruction tuning** — fine-tune (connector + LLM, sometimes ViT) on multimodal instruction data (LLaVA-Instruct, generated by prompting a text LLM with image annotations). Many frontier models now do **native multimodal pretraining** (joint from scratch) — see [Multimodal Pretraining](01_multimodal_pretraining.md).

**Visual token budget.** With patch size $p$, an $H\times W$ image yields $HW/p^2$ tokens before pooling — e.g., a 2,576 px square image at $p=14$ is ~$3.4\times10^4$ patches — so high-resolution screenshots/documents force pooling, tiling with downsampled global views, or token merging.

## Key Challenges
- **Resolution & token budget.** High-res images produce many patch tokens (cost ∝ tokens²); document/chart understanding needs high resolution. Tiling/"any-resolution" (AnyRes), token compression, and dynamic resolution address this.
- **Visual hallucination.** VLMs describe objects not present or misread details, often over-relying on the language prior (see [Hallucination](../05_alignment_and_safety/01_hallucination_causes_and_mitigations.md)).
- **OCR/fine-grained perception.** Reading small text, dense charts, and precise spatial relations is hard for CLIP-style global features.
- **GUI grounding for agents.** Computer use requires precise localization of small UI elements and multi-step state tracking across screenshots; benchmark saturation (OSWorld) hides long-tail failures.
- **Modality gap.** Image and text embeddings occupy separate cones in the shared space (see [Cross-Modal Alignment](02_cross_modal_alignment.md)).
- **Data scarcity/quality.** High-quality multimodal instruction data is harder to source than text.

## Solutions & Current Best Practices
**Default open recipe (connector style):** SigLIP/SigLIP 2 ViT + **MLP projector** + strong LLM, with **any-resolution tiling**, two-stage training (alignment → visual instruction tuning), and **high-quality synthetic instruction data**. Use **SigLIP** (sigmoid loss) over CLIP for better encoders. For efficiency, **compress visual tokens** (resampler/pooling). **Frontier and flagship open models** now train vision **natively** into the base model, though a small dedicated encoder (1–3B parameters) feeding a trillion-parameter MoE remains common (Mistral Large 3/4). For agents, train on screenshot→action trajectories with RL in parallel virtual desktops. Evaluate on MMMU, MathVista, DocVQA, ChartQA, hallucination benchmarks (POPE), and OSWorld-Verified/OSWorld 2.x for computer use.

## Latest Developments (2023–Nov 2026)
**Native multimodal, any-to-any** models (GPT-4o, Gemini) that handle text/image/audio/video in one model and can *generate* across modalities. **Any-resolution** and dynamic token allocation improved document/OCR/chart tasks. **Video** understanding via long context (Gemini). **Vision for agents** — GUI/computer-use models that perceive screenshots and act. **SigLIP/SigLIP2** replaced CLIP as the preferred encoder. Persistent focus on reducing **visual hallucination** and improving fine-grained perception.

**Mid-2025 → Nov 2026 update:**
- **Vision folds into flagship open MoEs (2026-02 → 2026-09).** Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (2026-02, Apache 2.0) is natively vision-language; Kimi K3 (2026-07; 2.8T total / 104B active, 3:1 KDA:MLA hybrid) ships native vision with 1M context; DeepSeek **V4.1-Flash** (2026-09, 552B MoE, text+image in, text out, MIT) replaced both V4-Flash and the experimental V4-Flash-Vision — the separate "-VL" model line is disappearing at the top end.
- **…but connectors survive at trillion scale.** Mistral Large 3 (2025-12; 675B/41B) uses a 2.5B vision encoder and Mistral Large 4 (2026-10; ~1.05T/52B) a 1.6B encoder — well under 1% of parameters — showing the encoder+projector design remains competitive when the LM is large.
- **Hosted ≠ open checkpoint.** Qwen3.8's open weights (2026-08, 2.4T/95B) are text-only while the hosted Qwen3.8-Max-Preview is multimodal; buyers of "open" models must check which modalities were actually released.
- **Computer use becomes a headline capability (2026-03 → 2026-09).** GPT-5.4 made native computer use general; OSWorld-Verified reached ~78–79% by 2026-04 (GPT-5.5, Opus 4.7) versus a ~72% human baseline on the original split, so labs moved to OSWorld 2.x with partial credit (Opus 5.5 81.8%; Haiku 5.5 72.4% on an offline subset — vendor). Agentic RL on thousands of parallel virtual desktops (ComputerRL, arXiv:2508.14040) drives these gains in the open.
- **Higher native resolution.** Opus 4.7 accepts images up to 2,576 px, reflecting dense-screenshot and document workloads where downsampling destroys small text.
- **Edge multimodality.** Gemma 4 (2026-04, Apache 2.0) E2B/E4B edge models use per-layer embeddings and take text, image and audio with 128K context; MiMo-V2.5 (2026-04, MIT) adds native vision and audio at ~310.8B/15B active with 1M context.
- **Vision-language-action.** Gemini Robotics 2 (2026-07) is a VLA for whole-body humanoid control, paired with the ER 2 planner and multi-robot task splitting (secondary reports).

## Interview Angles
> 💡 **What labs actually ask:**
- **"How does CLIP work and why is it used as a VLM encoder?"** Contrastive InfoNCE over image-text pairs → shared, language-aligned embedding space; features transfer well to LLM connectors.
- **"Projection vs cross-attention connectors — tradeoffs?"** Projection (LLaVA): simple, data-efficient, all visual tokens enter the sequence; cross-attention (Flamingo): keeps LLM frozen, scales to many images, more complex.
- **"Why do VLMs hallucinate visually?"** Language-prior over-reliance, weak fine-grained perception, low resolution.
- **"How do you handle high-resolution images?"** Tiling/any-resolution + visual-token compression (resampler/pooling); compute the token count $HW/p^2$ and the quadratic attention cost.
- **"Native multimodal vs bolt-on encoder — why does Gemini differ?"** Joint pretraining yields tighter cross-modal integration and generation, vs connecting pretrained unimodal models.
- **"Why would a lab attach a 1.6B vision encoder to a 1T-parameter MoE instead of training vision natively?"** Reuses a strong pretrained encoder, decouples vision and LM training schedules, keeps text pretraining cheap and stable; the cost is looser integration and no native image generation.
- **"What makes computer use harder than VQA?"** Tiny-target grounding, long-horizon state tracking over many screenshots, action-space design (API vs GUI), and the need for RL in interactive environments rather than static supervision.

## Open Problems
Robust **fine-grained perception** (OCR, dense charts, precise spatial/temporal reasoning), eliminating **visual hallucination**, efficient **high-resolution and long-video** processing, reliable **GUI grounding** over long agentic sessions, and truly unified **any-to-any** understanding+generation are open. Whether to build native-multimodal from scratch vs connect strong unimodal models is an unresolved architectural debate — 2026 flagships do both.
