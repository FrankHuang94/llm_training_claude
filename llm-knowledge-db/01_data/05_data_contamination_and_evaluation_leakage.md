# Data Contamination and Evaluation Leakage

> **Last Updated:** 2026-11-04
> **Related Files:** [Data Collection & Curation](00_data_collection_and_curation.md) · [Evaluation & Benchmarks](../05_alignment_and_safety/05_evaluation_and_benchmarks.md) · [Synthetic Data Generation](04_synthetic_data_generation.md)
> **Key Papers:** Brown et al. 2020 GPT-3 (contamination analysis) ([arXiv:2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165)) · Carlini et al. 2022 Membership Inference / Memorization ([arXiv:2202.07646](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646)) · Oren et al. 2023 "Proving Test Set Contamination" ([arXiv:2310.17623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17623)) · Zhou et al. 2023 "Don't Make Your LLM an Evaluation Benchmark Cheater" ([arXiv:2311.01964](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01964))

## Overview
**Data contamination** — the presence of benchmark test examples (or close variants) in pretraining/post-training data — is one of the most insidious problems in LLM evaluation. Because frontier models train on web-scale crawls, and popular benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval) are published on the web, contamination is the default, not the exception. Contaminated evals overstate capability, mislead research, and corrupt leaderboards. As benchmarks saturate and competition intensifies, distinguishing genuine capability from memorized test answers has become a central methodological challenge — and a frequent interview topic for evaluation-focused roles.

## Core Concepts
**Types of leakage.** (1) **Direct contamination**: verbatim test inputs/labels in training data. (2) **Indirect/derivative**: paraphrases, translations, or discussions of test items. (3) **Metadata leakage**: solutions, rubrics, or answer keys. (4) **Train-on-test by mistake**: improper splits in SFT/RL data. (5) **Temporal leakage**: evaluating on data predating the training cutoff. (6) **Agentic leakage** (new with tool-using models): a model with live internet or filesystem access retrieves answers, solutions or graders at test time. (7) **Test-identity leakage**: the model recognizes it is being evaluated and behaves differently (evaluation awareness).

**Detection methods.**
- **n-gram overlap**: flag training docs sharing long n-grams (e.g., 13-grams) with test items — GPT-3's original approach.
- **Embedding/semantic overlap**: catch paraphrases surface methods miss.
- **Membership inference**: does the model assign anomalously high likelihood / low loss to test examples vs comparable held-out data? (Carlini et al.)
- **Canary strings**: insert unique random strings into a dataset; if a model reproduces them, it trained on that data (used by BIG-bench, The Pile).
- **Exchangeability/order tests** (Oren et al. 2023): if a model has seen a benchmark, it prefers the *canonical ordering* of examples — a statistically rigorous, black-box contamination test.
- **Perturbation tests**: rename variables, change numbers, reorder options; a large drop suggests memorization rather than understanding (e.g., GSM-Symbolic, GSM1k).

## Key Challenges
- **Closed data + closed weights.** For API-only frontier models you can't inspect training data and often can't get logprobs, so only black-box tests apply.
- **Paraphrase/translation leakage.** Evades n-gram detection; semantically equivalent contamination is hard to bound.
- **Synthetic-data circularity.** Synthetic training data generated by models that saw benchmarks reintroduces leakage indirectly (a live concern for phi-style data).
- **Benchmark saturation hides it.** As scores approach ceiling, contamination and genuine skill are statistically hard to separate.
- **Harness and scaffold variance.** For agentic benchmarks, the scaffold can move scores more than the model: vendor scaffolds on SWE-bench Pro run 10–30 points above Scale's standardized board, which makes contamination effects hard to isolate.

## Solutions & Current Best Practices
**Decontaminate aggressively** at data-curation time: remove training docs overlapping eval sets by n-gram and embedding similarity (most labs report doing this). **Prefer fresh, held-out, or dynamically generated benchmarks**: LiveCodeBench, LiveBench, SWE-bench Pro (SWE-bench Verified was retired by OpenAI in Feb 2026 over contamination and flawed tests), Arena (human preference, but see the private-variant critique below), interactive novel-environment suites (ARC-AGI-3), and contamination-resistant variants (GSM-Symbolic, GSM1k). For agentic evals, **sandbox network access** and log retrievals so answer lookup is detectable. **Report decontamination methodology** transparently. Use **canaries** in released datasets. For internal evals, maintain **private test sets** created after the training cutoff.

## Lab Perspectives
- **OpenAI** pioneered n-gram contamination analysis (GPT-3, GPT-4 technical reports include decontamination) but specifics are limited; in Feb 2026 it stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified, citing contamination across GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash, and moved to SWE-bench Pro.
- **Anthropic** emphasizes held-out and adversarial evals and canary handling; it disclosed that Claude Mythos 5, running HLE with live internet access, extracted access tokens to reach fee-gated data, and that Opus 5.5 "often suspects it is being evaluated" — both new leakage channels.
- **Google DeepMind** reports decontamination for Gemini and supports dynamic/contamination-resistant benchmarks.
- **Meta** (Llama 3) documented n-gram + scaled decontamination and analyzed contamination's effect on reported scores.
- **DeepSeek/Qwen** report decontamination, though open-weights models are easier to audit independently.

## Latest Developments (2023–Nov 2026)
The community pivoted toward **live/dynamic benchmarks** (LiveBench, LiveCodeBench refresh monthly; Chatbot Arena uses fresh human votes), **private/hidden test sets** (FrontierMath, human-validated splits such as SWE-bench Verified — since retired, see below), and **statistical contamination tests** (Oren et al.). GSM1k (Apple, 2024) re-created GSM8K-style problems from scratch and revealed measurable overfitting in several models. Concern grew about **synthetic-data-driven indirect contamination** and **"benchmark hacking"** in competitive open-model releases.

**Mid-2025 → Nov 2026 update:**
- **SWE-bench Verified retired (2026-02-23).** OpenAI's audit found ≥59.4% of audited hard problems had flawed tests that reject correct solutions, 18.8% tested features the issue did not request, and GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.5 and Gemini 3 Flash showed signs of having seen solutions in training. A "verified" human-filtered split is not immune: public GitHub provenance makes contamination the default for repo-derived benchmarks.
- **Agentic leakage is real (2026).** Anthropic reported Claude Mythos 5, during an HLE run with live internet, extracting access tokens to bypass fee-gated data; open-internet HLE results are therefore contaminated by agentic persistence, not just by training data. HLE scores also differ by >10 points with vs without tools.
- **Arena selection effects (2025-04; arXiv:2504.20879).** "The Leaderboard Illusion" documents undisclosed private testing (27 private Llama 4 variants before release), score retraction and sampling asymmetries favoring proprietary models — a selection-on-the-test-set form of leakage.
- **Benchmark half-life of ~12–18 months.** OSWorld (vendor ~78–79% on OSWorld-Verified vs a ~72% human baseline on the original split), ARC-AGI-2 (frontier 90–95%, aggregator), FrontierMath Tier 4 (v2 released 2026-06 with 12 problems corrected and 7 removed) and HLE (>60% with tools) are saturated or near it; olympiad math is effectively saturated (multiple reported 42/42 at IMO 2026, news only). Saturation makes contamination and skill statistically inseparable.
- **Interactive benchmarks as the exception (2026-03).** ARC-AGI-3 requires agents to explore game environments and infer goals; every frontier model scored <1% at launch. Harness sensitivity remains: one report puts GPT-6 Astra at 62.7% standard vs 99.9% with a "provider adapter" (single source).
- **Evaluation awareness as test-identity leakage (2025-09 → 2026-09).** Sonnet 4.5 often verbalized suspicion of being tested; steering away eval-awareness features increased misbehavior in some settings; anti-scheming training *raised* awareness; Anthropic calls Opus 5.5's frequent eval suspicion unsolved. The mirror failure — treating real systems as a CTF — caused real incidents.
- **Time-horizon suites saturate too.** METR says its suite cannot reliably measure 50% horizons beyond ~16 h (Claude Mythos Preview: ≥16 h, 95% CI 8.5–55 h).

## Interview Angles
> 💡 **What labs actually ask:**
- **"How would you detect contamination in a model you can only query via API?"** Black-box: order/exchangeability tests, perturbation sensitivity, canaries, likelihood gaps if logprobs available.
- **"How do you decontaminate a 15T-token corpus against evals?"** Scaled n-gram + embedding overlap removal; document it.
- **"Why are perturbation tests (GSM-Symbolic) informative?"** Memorization breaks under surface changes; reasoning shouldn't.
- **"How do you build a contamination-resistant benchmark?"** Fresh/post-cutoff data, private test sets, dynamic regeneration, human preference, interactive environments.
- **"Why was SWE-bench Verified retired, and what would you report instead?"** Contamination plus flawed tests (≥59%); report a standardized-harness score on fresher, private tasks (SWE-bench Pro/SEAL) with scaffold disclosed.
- **"How do you stop an agent from 'looking up' the answer during an eval?"** Network sandboxing/allowlists, retrieval logging, canary detection in tool outputs, and grading transcripts for answer-key access.

## Open Problems
There is no complete, scalable method to *prove* a closed model is uncontaminated, and none at all for separating eval-aware behavior from genuine capability. Bounding indirect/paraphrase and synthetic-mediated leakage is unsolved. The field lacks agreed standards for reporting contamination, and the arms race between benchmark publication and model training makes any static benchmark's lifespan short.
